# Hardware & Runtime Optimisation Techniques in Prahari

This inventory lists every optimisation technique relevant to Arm edge deployment of Prahari,
strictly separating **(A) shipped and measured**, **(B) runtime tuning knobs supported by the
benchmark harness**, **(C) experimental / unmerged work**, and **(D) techniques intentionally not
used**. Every quantitative claim traces to [`docs/perf/README.md`](README.md) and
[`docs/perf/methodology.md`](methodology.md).

## A. Shipped & Measured Optimisations (Active on `main`)

| Technique | Exact Implementation | Measured Effect (Traced to Artifacts) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. 4-bit Group-32 QLoRA Quantisation + 8-bit Dynamic Activations + 8-bit Embeddings (`QLORA_INT4_EO8`)** | Official Meta ExecuTorch export `Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct-QLORA_INT4_EO8.pte` (`models/registry.json`: `llama32-1b-qlora`). 4-bit symmetric group-32 weights (`qsi4c32p`), per-token dynamic 8-bit activations (`qai8dxp`), 8-bit quantized embeddings/output (`EO8`), QLoRA-adapted for quality retention. | Cuts model size to **1.100 GiB** (`1,181,451,008 B`, ~2.1× smaller than an FP16 copy of the 1.24 B-parameter model, ≈2.47 GB computed, not measured), fitting comfortably inside a smartphone's memory budget (**1.66 GiB** PSS on Galaxy S23). Selected over `llama32-1b-spinquant` after Oracle N1 testing measured QLoRA faster (`~80–83` prefill / `~20–21` decode tok/s vs SpinQuant `58–63` prefill / `12–13.5` decode tok/s under mixed load). |
| **2. ExecuTorch + XNNPACK Delegate with Arm KleidiAI Micro-Kernels (`i8mm` & `dotprod`)** | Linux uses `executorch==1.6.0` (`TextLLMRunner` + XNNPACK delegate); Android uses `org.pytorch:executorch-android:1.5.1` (`LlmModule` + `libexecutorch.so` `arm64-v8a` XNNPACK, plus CI source-build option for ExecuTorch 1.6.0 with `-DEXECUTORCH_XNNPACK_ENABLE_KLEIDI=ON`). | **Neoverse-V2 `perf record` directly confirmed dispatch:** `kai_*` KleidiAI kernels consume **54.17%** of whole-process CPU time (`23.27%` `neon_i8mm` 4-bit prefill matmul + `20.57%` `neon_dotprod` 4-bit decode matvec + `5.63%` 8-bit `dotprod` + `3.97%` weight packing + `0.73%` activation quant/pack). Achieves **46.7 tok/s** (2T) and **104.6 tok/s** (8T) on Neoverse-V2, and **38.6–43.4 tok/s** on physical Galaxy S23 (`arm64-v8a`). |
| **3. Hybrid Two-Stage Architecture (Classical Detectors First, Asynchronous Llama Advisory Second)** | Stage 1 runs lightweight local scikit-learn / portable NumPy models + deterministic mechanism rules (`candidates/BEST/triage/detect.py`, `mech.py`; `android/python/mobile.py`) before invoking Llama. Stage 1 is authoritative for verdict, score, evidence, and vetted safety steps; Llama only writes a 1–2 sentence plain-language explanation. On Android (`MainActivity.kt:355–401`), Stage 1 renders the verdict and vetted steps immediately while Stage 2 runs asynchronously on `Bridge.worker`. | Keeps classification completely out of the LLM critical path: Stage 1 processes **206–215 SMS/s, 1,996–2,375 URL/s, 57–64 Indic SMS/s and 5,342–7,118 flows/s** in batch on Neoverse-N1 (round-3 `ctl-conf51-gen51` ledger, including process start) and **5–17 ms** warm on Galaxy S23 (vs `1.20–1.50 s` warm total with Llama). The verdict and vetted steps do not wait for Llama (UI time-to-visible is not separately instrumented; the 5–17 ms figure is the detector call alone), and detector artefacts total just **4.33 MiB** (Linux joblibs). |
| **4. Evidence-First Prompt Compaction** | `candidates/BEST/triage/llm.py` strips boilerplate, compresses detector evidence into a single concrete opening phrase (`compact_evidence`), neutralises live URLs (`example[.]top`), caps input snippets at 320 chars, and uses a terse 24-token system prompt. | On Oracle Neoverse-N1 (`research/evals/*/details.json`), compacting median prompt length from **222 → 199 → 137 → 130 tokens** (and median output **37 → 34 → 29 → 28 tokens**) reduced end-to-end latency from **5.39 s → 4.72 s → 4.00 s → 3.67 s/item** (−31.9% wall time; mixed host load caveat applies, token reduction is deterministic) while *improving* explanation quality score `E` (`0.8875 → 0.9473` in round 2). |
| **5. Bounded Greedy Decoding, Dynamic `seqLen`, & KV-Cache + CPU Flash SDPA** | Greedy decoding (`temperature=0.0`, zero beam/sampling search overhead), hard output cap `max_new_tokens=40` on Linux (`config.json`) and 40-callback stop (`module.stop()`) on Android (`MainActivity.kt:472`). `.pte` is exported with `use_kv_cache=1`, `use_sdpa_with_kv_cache=1` (dispatches `cpu_flash_attention<float, 32, 512>`), `enable_dynamic_shape=1`, `max_context_len=2048`. Android clamps `seqLen` per prompt to `[256, 2048]` (`MainActivity.kt:483`). | Caps decoding at about 40 steps. At the observed decode rates that is roughly 0.9 s on S23, 0.85 s on V2 2T and 2.0 s on N1; these are estimates from measured throughput, not guaranteed bounds (rates vary with thermals and load, and Linux may retry a failed generation). Greedy sampling costs 0–3 ms per item in total on V2 (6 items) (`Sampling time` lines in `evidence/neoverse-v2/out/offline-proof.txt`). Note: Android 1.5.1 AAR does not forward `LlmGenerationConfig.maxNewTokens` natively, so the 40-callback stop is an approximate guard rather than a strict token cap. |
| **6. Single-Instance Model Preload & Weight-Packing Reuse** | Android `MainActivity.kt` (`requestModel()` / `Bridge.module`) loads `LlmModule` once on a dedicated background worker and reuses it across checks. Linux `app/server.py` keeps a process-wide `_ex` singleton (`Explainer`); `bin/explain` loads once per batch. | Amortises the one-time `.pte` load (ExecuTorch `Model Load Time` 1.85–2.37 s in 11 of 12 V2 runs) and XNNPACK weight packing (`kai_run_rhs_pack_*`, `3.97%` CPU share in a 6-item batch). On Neoverse-V2 (2T), avoiding reload drops per-check latency from **4.02–4.06 s** (cold item 1) to **1.04–1.74 s** (warm items 2–6; mean **1.265 s**). On Galaxy S23, warm KYC checks run in **1.20–1.21 s** with **499–510 ms** TTFT. |
| **7. Dependency-Free Portable Detector Export on Android** | `android/tools/export_models.py` exports the four scikit-learn 1.9.1 joblib models into pure JSON weights (`android/python/portable.py` + NumPy 1.26.2), eliminating scikit-learn, SciPy, and joblib from the Android APK and avoiding cross-version pickle incompatibility on Python 3.10 arm64. | Keeps the `arm64-v8a` APK to **45.75 MiB** (portable JSONs = `3.30 MiB` compressed / `14.00 MiB` uncompressed), preserves exact parity against reference fixtures, and achieves **5–17 ms** warm Stage-1 classification on Galaxy S23 (`183–255 ms` on first cold load of a model JSON). |

## B. Runtime Tuning Knobs (Benchmarked / Supported in Harness, Not Hardcoded in App Defaults)

| Technique | Current State in Codebase | Measured Effect / Quantified Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **1. XNNPACK Threadpool Sizing & CPU Affinity Pinning** | `bench/common.py` and `bin/eval` set `OMP_NUM_THREADS=2` for detectors. `candidates/BEST/triage/llm.py` leaves ExecuTorch at its default threadpool size; `bench/perf/arm_bench.py` supports `--threads` (pre-load `_unsafe_reset_threadpool(n)`, strictly before `.pte` load per ExecuTorch issue #24765) and `--pin-cpus` (`taskset -c`). Android ExecuTorch 1.5.1 uses built-in `cpuinfo` big-core detection (no Java pinning API). | On Arm Neoverse-V2 (1 → 2 → 4 → 8 threads, 3 repeats × 6 items): warm prefill scales **112.8 → 221.6 → 412.2 → 751.2 tok/s (6.66×)**, warm decode scales **25.7 → 46.7 → 76.0 → 104.3 tok/s (4.06×)**, warm TTFT drops **1,060 → 540 → 290 → 159 ms**, and warm item wall drops **2.317 → 1.265 → 0.777 → 0.534 s**. |
| **2. Detector-First Gating (Skip Llama on `NO RISK FOUND` Items)** | **Android: on by default** (About → *Explain NO RISK results with Llama*, off). A result shown as NO RISK gets the detector's fixed text (the prompt's required opening plus its vetted key fact, checked by `validate()`; `android/python/mobile.py` `vetted_explanation`) and no Llama call; DANGEROUS, SUSPICIOUS and app-relabelled bank alerts take the unchanged Llama path. The card says the text is the detector's. `candidates/BEST/bin/explain` still explains every item. `bench/perf/arm_bench.py --gating-mode flagged-only` implements the same policy on Linux; `adb_bench.py run --gating-mode both` measures it on a phone. | **Measured on the S23 (APK from `5b59ee2`, PR #75):** two gated benign checks took **0.186 s** and **0.312 s** ([`s23-gating-20261124.md`](s23-gating-20261124.md)); n = 2, so the projection below is still arithmetic. Arithmetic projection, from the measured stage costs: a cleared item would cost the detector time (**5–17 ms** warm on S23; under 20 ms per item in batch on N1 for every task) instead of the full check (**1.20–1.50 s** warm on S23). For a hypothetical inbox that is 90% benign, mean cost per message would fall from ~1.25 s to roughly 0.1 × 1.25 s ≈ 0.13 s. Detection verdicts are unchanged because Stage 1 alone decides them. |

## D. Techniques Intentionally NOT Used (Design Trade-offs)

1. **Cross-request prompt / KV-cache prefix caching:** **Deliberately disabled.** Both Linux
   (`self.runner.reset()` in `triage/llm.py:575`) and Android (`module.resetContext()` in
   `Runner.generate` in `MainActivity.kt`, before and after every generate) clear KV state before every single check. Because inputs are untrusted
   SMS/URL strings that may contain prompt-injection attempts, resetting context per item prevents
   cross-message contamination at a small cost (~24 system-prompt tokens ≈ `~32 ms` of prefill at the measured
   751 tok/s on V2 8T, ≈ `~110 ms` at 221.6 tok/s on V2 2T; S23 prefill speed is now logged by the app per check but not yet measured on the phone).
2. **Vendor-locked NPU/GPU delegates (QNN / Vulkan / CoreML):** **Not used in Phase 1.** Targeting
   ExecuTorch's CPU XNNPACK + KleidiAI backend guarantees one portable `.pte` binary runs identically
   across the Armv8.2 and Armv9 targets we measured (Neoverse-N1, Neoverse-V2, Snapdragon 8 Gen 2)
   with no per-SoC recompilation. Other Arm SoCs (MediaTek, Exynos, Raspberry Pi) were not tested.
3. **LLM-based classification:** **Rejected by design.** Running Llama as the classifier would add
   `>1.2 s` per message even on benign traffic, hurt precision on Indian scam edge cases, and make
   verdicts vulnerable to prompt injection. Keeping classification in `4.33 MiB` local detectors +
   rules is faster, deterministic, and injection-resistant.
