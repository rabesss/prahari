## Executive summary

- Build separate, small detection paths for SMS, URLs, and network flows. Use rules and calibrated classifiers for decisions; let Llama produce a short explanation from recorded detector signals. [inferred]
- Start with character n-gram TF-IDF plus logistic regression for SMS and lexical URL features plus a small linear/tree model. These are cheap and expose useful evidence, but random-split results do not establish performance on current Indian scams. [sourced: https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics15040894] [inferred]
- Add Hinglish/Hindi and Indian scam examples to evaluation early. Public Indian-context examples are small and partly hand-written; UCI’s larger SMS set is old and mostly non-Indian. [sourced: https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/karanverma19/Indian_Multilingual_Scam_Message_Dataset] [sourced: https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/bolewara/hinglish-scam-text-dataset] [sourced: https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/228/sms+spam+collection]
- Compact BERT variants and quantization are plausible options, but available papers do not establish that a transformer will beat a classical baseline on this specific Indian scam task. MobileBERT reports 62 ms on a Pixel 4; this is not a Pi 5 or ExecuTorch measurement. [sourced: https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.195/] [inferred]
- ExecuTorch 1.6.0 has Linux aarch64 wheels on PyPI. Public ungated ExecuTorch PTE releases exist for Llama 3.2 1B, but Meta’s Llama 3.2 license still applies. [sourced: https://pypi.org/project/executorch/1.6.0/] [sourced: https://huggingface.co/api/models/executorch-community/Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct-SpinQuant_INT4_EO8-ET]
- I found a Pi 5 Q4 result for llama.cpp and a Pi 5 ExecuTorch INT8 result, but no directly comparable Pi 5 ExecuTorch 4-bit result. I found no measured result for Llama 3.2 1B 4-bit on this two-vCPU Neoverse N1 box. ## 2. Technical approaches for small Arm devices

### Suggested detector layout

Keep each input type on its own fast path: SMS text classifier, lexical URL detector, and flow-level network detector. Persist the decision, calibrated score, detector version, and fired evidence features; call Llama only to explain those facts. [inferred]

For an offline-first prototype, combine local rules for clear cases with a compact learned model for ambiguous cases. A threat-feed hit can be one rule, but it is a staleable positive-only signal, not a complete detector; lexical models are needed for unseen URLs. [inferred] [sourced: https://www.unb.ca/cic/datasets/url-2016.html]

### SMS and smishing

- A low-cost baseline is word TF-IDF unigrams/bigrams plus character TF-IDF 3–5 grams and logistic regression. Character features can retain signal from misspellings, punctuation changes, phone-like tokens, and code-mixed spellings; this exact setup has not been validated on Indian SMS. [sourced: https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics15040894] [inferred]
- On UCI SMS Spam Collection, a 2026 study reports character 3-gram TF-IDF plus logistic regression at 98.55% accuracy, 94.32% spam F1, 90.50% spam recall, and 0.9893 AUC under 10-fold cross-validation. The corpus is small and old, so treat this as a reproducible baseline, not a production estimate. [sourced: https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics15040894] [sourced: https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/228/sms+spam+collection]
- A 2026 edge-focused study reports a cost-sensitive BiGRU with masked pooling at 5.21 MB and 0.25 ms CPU model-only latency, compared with 256.02 MB and 40.78 ms for its DistilBERT reference; it reports better cross-domain smishing recall than TF-IDF + logistic regression in both transfer directions. These are that paper’s hardware/setup measurements. [sourced: https://doi.org/10.1080/24751839.2026.2713250]
- Compact transformer options include TinyBERT and MobileBERT, followed by INT8 quantization or structured pruning. MobileBERT reports 25.3M parameters, 77.7 GLUE, and 62 ms latency on a four-thread Pixel 4; the authors report 4x size reduction from 8-bit quantization with little benchmark loss. A 2026 TinyLite paper reports 81.6% fewer FLOPs with under 1.1 percentage points of F1 loss versus its BERT-Tiny baseline. These findings motivate experiments but do not establish Indian scam performance or ExecuTorch speed. [sourced: https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.195/] [sourced: https://doi.org/10.1109/most69733.2026.00042] [inferred]
- Keep features such as character n-grams, URL presence/host tokens, digit and currency ratios, urgency terms, requests for OTP/PIN/password, and impersonated services. Prefer these as explainable features or evidence instead of brittle block rules. [inferred]
- Evaluate English, Devanagari Hindi, Romanized Hindi/Hinglish, and code-mixed SMS separately. Normalize Unicode consistently but preserve raw text; avoid aggressive stopword removal or stemming that could erase cues such as “OTP”, “UPI”, or “abhi”. Split by source, campaign/template, sender, or time where possible to limit near-duplicate leakage. [inferred]
- Indian advisories describe recurring KYC-update/account-blocking pressure, UPI refund/transfer requests, electricity disconnection, parcel impersonation, lottery/reward claims, and demands for OTP/PIN or credentials. These are useful label and test categories, not a complete taxonomy. [sourced: https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_PressRelease.aspx?prid=57244] [sourced: https://www.financialservices.gov.in/beta/en/page/csft]

### Phishing URL detection

- Use URL-only features for offline operation: total and host length, subdomain count, digit/symbol ratios, @ and unusual ports, IP-literal hosts, punycode/Unicode indicators, percent escapes, path/query length, entropy, suspicious file extensions, and suspicious brand tokens. Parse host/path/query separately and do not fetch the target page during classification. [inferred]
- A low-cost baseline is character 3–5-gram TF-IDF plus logistic regression or a shallow boosted tree. Compare with hand-built lexical features and a small MLP. Keep cached reputation/blacklist matches as evidence, not the only detector. [inferred]
- PhiUSIIL contains 235,795 URLs (134,850 legitimate, 100,945 phishing), with 54 fields and a 54.2 MB CSV. Its paper reports 99.24% for fully incremental training and 99.79% for pre-training on that dataset; those in-dataset scores do not establish resilience on later domains. [sourced: https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/967/phiusiil+phishing+url+dataset] [sourced: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.cose.2023.103545]
- A recent adversarial URL evaluation reports clean accuracy of 0.9962–0.9984 across four model families, but subdomain injection reduced LR, XGBoost, and CharCNN to about 0.432. Use temporal/domain-held-out splits and perturbation tests for added subdomains, homoglyphs, encoding, path changes, and query padding; a random clean split can overstate readiness. [sourced: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1834407/pdf] [inferred]

### Network intrusion detection

- For Pi-class CPU budgets, begin with flow summaries rather than payload inspection: duration, packet/byte counts, packet-size statistics, inter-arrival times, direction ratios, protocol, TCP flags, and rates. Compare a small boosted tree, shallow random forest, logistic model, or selected-feature anomaly detector with heavier neural models. [sourced: https://www.unb.ca/cic/datasets/ids-2017.html] [sourced: https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/9/1/144] [inferred]
- A Raspberry Pi IoT IDS paper reports that selecting only a small number of attack-specific features can make ML detectors practical on Raspberry Pi with little performance loss in that study. This does not establish performance on home-router traffic or the challenge device. [sourced: https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/9/1/144]
- A 2026 CIC-IDS2017 calibration study reports duplicate-grouped evaluation and tuned XGBoost at F1 0.9978, ROC-AUC 0.99996, and Brier 0.00070; Platt scaling reduced ECE from 0.000606 to 0.000190. The authors also note sparse confidence regions and shortcut-learning concerns. [sourced: http://urn.kb.se/resolve?urn=urn%3Anbn%3Ase%3Amiun%3Adiva-57741]
- Report macro-F1, per-attack recall, false-positive rate, and alert volume, not accuracy alone. Split by capture day/scenario and drop identifiers, timestamps, and other label proxies before training. CIC-IDS2017 has flow-extraction and labeling weaknesses, so add a second dataset or controlled local capture before making generalization claims. [sourced: https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3486622.3493973] [sourced: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36039] [inferred]

### Calibration, abstention, and robustness

- Tune thresholds against the actual harm trade-off: missed scam versus false warning, and missed intrusion versus alert fatigue. Calibrate on held-out data with Platt or temperature scaling, then inspect reliability plots, Brier score, and ECE by language/source/scam family. Calibration is not a guarantee under distribution shift. [sourced: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-023-06336-7] [sourced: http://urn.kb.se/resolve?urn=urn%3Anbn%3Ase%3Amiun%3Adiva-57741] [inferred]
- Add an abstain/review state for low-confidence or out-of-distribution cases. Report risk versus coverage so the team can show how errors change as the detector defers more cases. [sourced: https://arxiv.org/html/2208.12084]
- Test spelling swaps, spacing, zero-width/homoglyph characters, shorteners, subdomain abuse, urgency wording, and language mixing. Use held-out campaigns and native-speaker review; synthetic augmentations alone do not prove robustness to real attackers. [sourced: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1834407/pdf] [inferred]

## 4. ExecuTorch on aarch64 Linux and Llama 3.2 1B

### Linux aarch64 wheel

As of 9 November 2026, PyPI lists ExecuTorch 1.6.0, released 4 November, with CPython 3.11–3.15 manylinux_2_28_aarch64 wheels. The CPython 3.11 artifact is executorch-1.6.0-cp311-cp311-manylinux_2_28_aarch64.whl; the release lists cp312–cp315 equivalents. Check target glibc and Python ABI before deployment. [sourced: https://pypi.org/project/executorch/1.6.0/]

The PyPI description says the wheel does not bundle PyTorch; its prebuilt runtime includes core ATen and XNNPACK with backend/operator limits. For the LLM C++ runner and Python bindings, the documented route builds the needed runner and pybind components from ExecuTorch source. ### Export and quantization

The official LLM exporter supports Llama 3.2 1B. The documented Llama route supplies the Meta-format checkpoint and params file, uses model class llama3_2, and writes metadata such as BOS/EOS IDs for the runner. The standard exporter supports KV cache, SDPA with KV cache, prompt/context limits, XNNPACK delegation, and 8-bit dynamic activations plus 4-bit groupwise weights (8da4w). [sourced: https://docs.pytorch.org/executorch/stable/llm/export-llm.html]

For an initial export, use the XNNPACK Llama config with 8da4w and a moderate group size, quantize embeddings where supported, set a realistic context maximum, and enable recommended KV cache options. Regular PTQ can hurt Llama 3.2 1B quality; the guide highlights SpinQuant and QAT+LoRA as better-quality 4-bit recipes. [sourced: https://docs.pytorch.org/executorch/stable/llm/export-llm.html] [sourced: https://github.com/pytorch/executorch/tree/main/examples/models/llama] [inferred]

SpinQuant and QAT+LoRA require compatible quantized checkpoints and export configs; they are not simply switches that create those training improvements from any downloaded model. A published compatible PTE avoids training/export work for Phase 1. [sourced: https://github.com/pytorch/executorch/tree/main/examples/models/llama] [inferred]

### Export RAM and KV cache

The official docs and benchmarks found do not publish a minimum peak RAM for exporting Llama 3.2 1B to XNNPACK PTE. The 1B PTE/runtime sizes are not export peak measurements; a 3B export issue reports OOM on a 32 GB host, but that does not establish a 1B minimum. Treat 11 GB as plausible but unverified for a local 1B export, and measure peak RSS/swap if attempting it. [unknown] [sourced: https://github.com/pytorch/executorch/issues/8725]

Llama 3.2 1B has 16 layers, 8 key/value heads, and head dimension 64. For batch 1, the cache holds 16 × 2 × 8 × 64 = 16,384 values/token: about 32 KiB/token FP16 or 64 KiB/token FP32, before allocator overhead. A 4,096-token cache is about 128 MiB FP16 or 256 MiB FP32; an 8-bit cache is smaller if supported by the chosen export. This is calculated from model configuration, not measured RSS. [sourced: https://huggingface.co/rasbt/llama-3.2-from-scratch/blob/main/model.py] [sourced: https://docs.pytorch.org/executorch/stable/llm/export-llm.html] [inferred]

For this assistant, 1,024–2,048 tokens and short outputs should be enough for one SMS/URL plus compact evidence; set the window explicitly to avoid allocating a long cache the task does not need. [inferred]

## 5. Keeping Llama explanations reliable

- Make the detector authoritative for threat label and score. Pass Llama typed evidence such as verdict, calibrated score, fired signals, suspicious span, and safe action; have it paraphrase those facts, never override them or invent an independent score. [inferred]
- Require a small structured result with enumerated fields, for example a JSON object containing risk, why, and action. Validate with a parser/schema; on invalid output, use a deterministic template. OWASP recommends constrained behavior, defined output formats, deterministic validation, and treating generated output as untrusted. [sourced: https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/] [inferred]
- Keep the explanation to one or two sentences, a fixed token cap, and one recommended action. Short inputs/outputs reduce latency and cache use. [inferred]
- Ground every explanation in detector evidence: say “This asks for KYC details through an unverified link” only if those features were extracted. If unsure, say the message cannot be confirmed and advise official-channel verification. RBI advises against suspicious KYC links and sharing OTPs/credentials. [sourced: https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_PressRelease.aspx?prid=57244] [inferred]
- Treat SMS, URLs, packet strings, OCR, and feed text as untrusted data, even when they say to ignore instructions. Keep them separate from system instructions and give the model no ability to open URLs, send messages, or execute remediation. Delimiters organize a prompt but are not a security boundary; OWASP says prompt-only fixes are not foolproof. [sourced: https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/] [sourced: https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html] [inferred]
- Validate/escape explanation text before rendering. Never let generated text trigger blocking, UPI operations, app installation, or a network request. OWASP recommends least privilege and validation outside the model. [sourced: https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/] [inferred]
- Use a fixed factual action list: do not click, do not share OTP/PIN, verify through the official bank/service app or number, and use 1930/cybercrime.gov.in where appropriate. RBI advisories support these actions; application logic should select them rather than letting Llama invent a procedure. [sourced: https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_PressRelease.aspx?prid=57244] [inferred]
