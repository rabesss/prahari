# Bharat AI-SoC Challenge judging research: PS3

**Research date:** 2026-11-09  
**Scope:** Public challenge materials and closely related Arm Developer Labs material. The proposed rubric below is a proxy for planning; it is not an official score sheet.

## Findings at a glance

- The 2026–27 official page publishes the phases, submissions, PS3 requirements and winner-selection format, but I found no phase-specific point weights or a formal judge score sheet. **[unknown: none of the public materials reviewed provided a percentage rubric or phase-by-phase scoring form.]**
- The clearest published judging categories are from the first edition's FAQ: problem understanding/alignment, technical correctness, novelty, Arm tools, performance/optimization, documentation, and demo/presentation. **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Challenge_Page.html + quote: “Understanding of the Problem Statement and Alignment to Program Objective”; “Technical Correctness”; “Novelty”; “Use of ARM Tools”; “Solution Performance and Optimization”; “Documentation Quality and Completeness”; “Solution Demo and Presentation”.]**
- A first-edition PS5 briefing video gives the closest quantitative evaluation analogue: completeness, performance and novelty, with accuracy, latency, resource use and power named as performance measures. This is a project-specific precedent, not an announced rubric for 2026–27 PS3. **[sourced: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ufBkxl1zxo + quote at 8:29: “three key aspects which are completeness, performance, and novelty”; at 8:46–8:53: “four metrics” are “accuracy, latency, resource utilization, and power consumption”.]**
- The second edition explicitly says students and working professionals/developers compete “on equal terms” and names one winner per problem statement after a live virtual demo and jury Q&A. **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Bharat_AI_SoC_2026_27.html + quote: “One winner for each problem statement is selected after the final live virtual demo and jury Q&A”; “Students (UG/PG), Working Professionals & Developers compete on equal terms across five problem statements.”]**

### Mandatory PS3 requirements are requirements, not published score weights

PS3 requires an on-device system covering threats such as network intrusions, malware behaviour, phishing/scam communications; it names network anomaly/intrusion monitoring, phishing URL and scam SMS detection, plain-language explanation, remediation suggestions and local processing. **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Bharat_AI_SoC_2026_27.html + quote: “Monitoring and classifying network traffic for anomalies and intrusions”; “Detecting phishing URLs”; “Detecting scam SMS messages”; “Explaining threats to non-expert users in plain language”; “Recommending remediation actions”; “Processing data locally on the device without relying on cloud services”.]**

For PS3, “A model from the Llama family must be used for the advisory or reasoning component and deployed on-device using ExecuTorch as the reference runtime.” **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Bharat_AI_SoC_2026_27.html + quote: “A model from the Llama family must be used for the advisory or reasoning component and deployed on-device using ExecuTorch as the reference runtime.”]** Treat this as a compliance gate in a proxy rubric; the official page does not state that Llama/ExecuTorch earns a specific number of points. **[inferred: mandatory technical wording supports checking compliance, while no published weight assigns it a score.]**

The official page lists Arm-based Android, Raspberry Pi 5 CPU-only, Raspberry Pi 5 with AI HAT, and Arm-based Linux devices as hardware options. **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Bharat_AI_SoC_2026_27.html + quote: “Arm-based Android smartphones”; “Raspberry Pi 5, CPU-only”; “Raspberry Pi 5 with AI HAT+ / AI HAT+ 2”; “Arm-based Linux devices”.]** The page instructs teams using Hailo to document separate HailoRT and ExecuTorch pipelines. **[sourced: same URL + quote: “These are separate pipelines and should be clearly documented.”]**

## 2. First-edition evidence and closest analogues

### First-edition judging categories

The current official second-edition page recounts the inaugural challenge as having “more than 3,000 students across 1,238 teams” and says “Following three levels of evaluation and virtual mentorship, 150 shortlisted teams were narrowed to five winners.” **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Bharat_AI_SoC_2026_27.html + exact quotes above.]** The public wording establishes the scale and three-level count but does not name those three evaluation levels or state how the 150-to-five selection was scored. **[unknown: the reviewed official source gives no stage names, weights or selection formula for the first edition.]**

The first-edition challenge page's FAQ Q10 is the most direct broad judging list. It says the panel “may look into” seven areas: understanding/alignment, technical correctness/implementation quality, novelty, use and criticality of Arm tools, performance/optimization, documentation/completeness, and demo/presentation. **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Challenge_Page.html + quote: “Some key aspects that the Panel of Judges may look into include:” followed by those seven bullets.]** It gives no numeric weights. **[unknown: no percentages or score scale appear in Q10.]**

That page's schedule says project submissions were evaluated by “industry & academic experts” and selected teams would give a “pitch” at the finals. **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Challenge_Page.html + quote: “The submitted projects will be evaluated by the industry & academic experts”; “Selected teams will be invited for a virtual meetup/hack to deliver a pitch”.]** Its announcement describes a “final virtual evaluation and project demonstration” and directs finalists to await detailed instructions by email. **[sourced: same URL + quote: “final virtual evaluation round”; “virtual evaluation and project demonstration”; “detailed instructions”.]** It does not disclose a scoring distinction between submission evaluation and final pitch. **[unknown: no stage-by-stage criteria or weights were located.]**

The page states “Top-5 teams” receive INR 1 lakh each and displays a “Winning Team” under each of the five problem statements. **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Challenge_Page.html + quote: “the Top-5 teams will receive a prize money of Indian Rupees (INR) One (1) Lakh each”; page headings: “Winning Team” under PS1–PS5.]** This supports five winners, apparently one in each PS; the new-edition page makes that rule explicit. **[inferred: five listed PS winners and five prizes indicate one winner per PS in the first edition.]**

**Date note:** although the question refers to the first edition as 2025, the live first-edition page's detailed timeline lists registration in January 2026, submissions in February 2026, evaluation through 5 March 2026 and finals in mid-March. **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Challenge_Page.html + quote/table entries: “05 Jan 2026”; “10 Feb 2026”; “05 Mar 2026”; “Mid of March”.]** The official second-edition page calls it the “inaugural” edition but does not provide a year for that edition in the sentence. **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Bharat_AI_SoC_2026_27.html + quote: “The inaugural Bharat AI-SoC Student Challenge”.]** The public materials reviewed therefore do not resolve whether “2025” refers to its launch/branding year or the competition run; the official page's listed competition activity is in 2026. **[unknown: no official source reviewed reconciled the year label with the listed dates.]**

### Closest published performance rubrics

1. **Arm Education's PS5 briefing video (closest explicit evaluation breakdown).** It says a custom dataset will be judged for “completeness, performance, and novelty.” Under performance it names “accuracy, latency, resource utilization, and power consumption”; accuracy is “average precision based on intersection over union.” **[sourced: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ufBkxl1zxo + quotes at 8:29–9:08.]** It expects “a minimum of 2X speedup” over CPU-only Arm execution and improvements in latency, throughput and power efficiency, plus efficient FPGA LUT/BRAM/DSP use. **[sourced: same URL + quotes at 6:16–6:35.]** It also expects a public Git repository, system architecture/design decisions and performance comparisons in documentation, and a live or recorded demo showing FPS/latency and prediction confidence. **[sourced: same URL + quotes at 6:47–8:13.]** This is a PS5-specific challenge briefing, so apply it as precedent for measurable engineering evidence, not as a PS3 2026–27 rule. **[inferred: PS5's FPGA/vision task and the later PS3 task are different problem statements and editions.]**

2. **First-edition PS3-like target in the challenge page.** Its former PS3 was road anomaly detection, not today's cybersecurity PS3. Its targets include “≥5 FPS near-real-time inference,” “High precision to reduce false positives in logging,” and robustness “under varying lighting conditions.” **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Challenge_Page.html + quote from former PS3 Performance Targets.]** These targets show that the challenge page used measurable performance goals, but they are not targets for the 2026–27 cybersecurity PS3. **[inferred: the topic and PS definition changed between editions.]**

3. **Arm Developer Labs Ethos-U85 project page.** Its closest general project-page analogue asks for quantitative reporting of “Inference latency, throughput (FPS or tokens/s), and memory footprint,” says power efficiency is optional, and asks for comparison with Ethos-U55/U65. **[sourced: https://github.com/arm-education/Arm-Developer-Labs/blob/main/Projects/Projects/Ethos-U85-NPU-Applications.md + quote under “Analysis and Benchmarking”.]** It asks for model deployment/optimization and a working application demonstration, but it is a project brief rather than a competition judging sheet. **[sourced: same URL + quotes: “Model Deployment and Optimization”; “Implement a working example”; “Analysis and Benchmarking”.]** The repository template similarly provides project-description, hardware/software/skills and resource headings, not a common judge-weight template. **[sourced: https://github.com/arm-education/Arm-Developer-Labs/blob/main/template.md + quote/headings: “Description”; “Hardware, Software and Skills Required”; “Resources”.]**

## 3. What the 2026–27 theme implies for PS3 evidence

The official theme says the shift is from “building AI applications to engineering AI-enabled products on SoCs”; its end-to-end journey names “AI algorithm development, Optimisation, System integration, Edge deployment, Benchmarking, Validation, Product engineering.” **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Bharat_AI_SoC_2026_27.html + exact quotes.]** The measures below translate that language to reviewable PS3 evidence; these are recommendations, not official contest thresholds. **[inferred: they operationalize the published theme and PS3 requirements into testable evidence.]**

| Theme element | Reviewable PS3 evidence to show | Candidate measurements |
|---|---|---|
| **AI algorithm development** | Separate threat detection for network intrusion/malware behavior, phishing URLs and scam SMS; explain which inputs/features trigger each result. The challenge names all these functions. **[sourced: official challenge URL + PS3 bullets: “network traffic for anomalies and intrusions”; “phishing URLs”; “scam SMS messages”.]** | Per-domain precision, recall, F1 and PR-AUC; macro average; confusion matrices; false positives per fixed volume of benign traffic/messages; calibration or abstention rate. **[inferred: these metrics make multi-class/detection quality and false-alert cost observable.]** |
| **Optimization** | Show a baseline and optimized model/runtime on the same Arm device; document quantization/model-size choices and accuracy cost. Arm's closest project brief asks for optimization and quantitative latency/throughput/memory reporting. **[sourced: Ethos-U85 project URL + quote: “Model Deployment and Optimization”; “Inference latency, throughput ... and memory footprint”.]** | Model/package size, peak RAM, p50/p95 inference latency, throughput, and accuracy delta versus unoptimized baseline; optionally power/energy. **[inferred: these quantify resource/performance tradeoffs on constrained edge hardware.]** |
| **System integration** | Demonstrate end-to-end ingestion → detector → evidence passed to Llama → plain-language explanation/remediation, including error handling. PS3 explicitly requires monitoring/detection plus explanation and remediation. **[sourced: official challenge URL + quotes from PS3 requirements above.]** | Automated end-to-end test pass rate; component and pipeline latency; supported input formats and failure/recovery tests. **[inferred: integration can be judged repeatably through full-path tests.]** |
| **Edge deployment** | Run on the selected Arm platform and demonstrate no cloud calls or network dependency; show Llama through ExecuTorch on-device. These are explicit PS3 conditions. **[sourced: official challenge URL + quotes: “entirely on Arm-based edge hardware without cloud dependency”; Llama ... “deployed on-device using ExecuTorch”.]** | Successful run with network disabled; packet/DNS capture showing no external calls; runtime/model provenance check; RAM and storage use. **[inferred: these tests evidence local execution rather than relying on architecture diagrams alone.]** |
| **Benchmarking** | Publish a repeatable benchmark and identify device, OS, runtime, model, test set and measurement method. The PS5 and Ethos-U85 analogues both ask for comparative/quantitative benchmark evidence. **[sourced: YouTube PS5 video URL + quote: “performance comparison between CPU-only solution and your solution”; Ethos-U85 project URL + “Report quantitative results”.]** | p50/p95 and worst-case latency, throughput, peak memory, CPU utilization, optional energy per event; compare CPU baseline, optimized path and quantization variants. **[inferred: reporting baselines and test conditions makes performance claims comparable.]** |
| **Validation** | Use held-out data and realistic benign/high-risk samples; test false positives, unseen variants and failure cases. The challenge calls for validation, but does not specify a dataset split or validation protocol. **[sourced: official challenge URL + theme item “Validation”.]** **[inferred: held-out and shift testing provide direct evidence for that theme element.]** | Held-out metrics by input class/source; false-positive rate at stated recall; robustness across message styles, URL obfuscations and traffic patterns; repeat runs with fixed test hashes. **[inferred: these expose generalization and alert burden beyond training-set results.]** |
| **Product engineering** | Make the alert understandable to non-experts and provide safe, prioritized remediation. Show the entire user workflow and how a human can inspect evidence before taking action. PS3 explicitly calls for plain-language explanations and recommendations. **[sourced: official challenge URL + quote: “Explaining threats to non-expert users in plain language”; “Recommending remediation actions”.]** | Task completion and comprehension review with representative users; percentage of explanations that cite detector evidence; safe-action checklist and sampled expert review. **[inferred: user comprehension, grounding and action safety are product qualities that raw detector accuracy cannot establish.]** |

## 4. Proposed weighted proxy rubric for a PS3 submission

The weights below total **100%** and combine the first edition's seven published categories with the second edition's on-SoC engineering theme and current PS3 requirements. They are a practical self-assessment rubric, not official weights. **[inferred: no official weights were published in the sources reviewed; the proposed allocation maps the named criteria to measurable PS3 evidence.]**

| Proxy criterion | Weight | What to score | Measurement mode |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Problem alignment and functional completeness | **15%** | Coverage of intrusions/malware behavior, phishing URLs and scam SMS; clear plain-language explanation and actionable remediation; complete application and required deliverables. | Automated feature/test checklist plus human review of completeness and fit. |
| Technical correctness and detection quality | **20%** | Held-out detection quality across the three main input families; false-alert burden; documented data and model choices. | **Mostly automatic:** compute metrics from labeled held-out sets. Human review for dataset validity and threat coverage. |
| Arm system integration and required technology compliance | **15%** | Runs on the chosen Arm device; Llama is genuinely used for advisory/reasoning through ExecuTorch; system components are integrated and documented. | **Mixed:** runtime/instrumentation checks can verify execution; human review of architecture, implementation quality and Arm-tool criticality. |
| Performance and optimization | **15%** | End-to-end p50/p95 latency, throughput, peak memory, model size, and optimized-versus-baseline comparison; power/energy if available. | **Mostly automatic:** repeatable on-device benchmark scripts and device/resource telemetry. |
| Offline operation, privacy and resilience | **10%** | Detection and advisory continue with networking disabled; no cloud dependency; error handling and recovery are demonstrated. | **Automatic:** network-disabled run, outbound-call/DNS checks and scripted failure tests. Human review of privacy design. |
| Explanation grounding and remediation safety | **10%** | Explanations point to detector evidence, avoid unsupported certainty, communicate risk plainly, and recommend safe next steps. | **Mixed:** schema/evidence-presence and action-policy checks can be automated; sample-by-sample correctness, clarity and safety need human review. |
| Novelty and product/social value | **8%** | A meaningful technical or user-experience contribution beyond a thin model wrapper; practical relevance to digital safety users. | **Human judgment:** compare against baselines and assess usefulness, originality and fit. |
| Documentation and reproducibility | **6%** | Reproducible setup, model/runtime versions, dataset provenance/splits, benchmark method, architecture and limitations. | **Mixed:** clean setup and benchmark reruns can be scripted; clarity and completeness need human review. |
| Demo, presentation and jury Q&A | **6%** | Show actual local operation, representative true/false cases, latency/resource evidence, and clear answers about limitations and tradeoffs. | **Human judgment:** demo quality, communication and Q&A. A timer or runtime overlay can substantiate technical claims. |
| **Total** | **100%** |  |  |

The first-edition FAQ names these same broad dimensions—alignment, technical correctness, novelty, Arm tools, performance, documentation and presentation—without weights; the PS5 video adds completeness/performance/novelty and explicit performance metrics. **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Challenge_Page.html + Q10 criteria; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ufBkxl1zxo + quote at 8:29 and 8:46–9:08.]** The additional offline/privacy and explanation/remediation weights above come from the current PS3 task statement, not a published scoring category. **[inferred: these rubric items operationalize PS3's required local processing, non-expert explanations and remediation.]**

### What can be measured automatically?

- **Detection accuracy / false positives:** yes, against fixed held-out labels; report per-domain metrics and fixed operating points, not only overall accuracy. **[inferred: the challenge's PS3 lists distinct detection jobs, so per-domain measurements avoid hiding a weak class.]**
- **Arm latency / throughput / memory:** yes, with repeatable runs on the actual Arm device; include warm/cold behavior and p50/p95 rather than one best run. **[inferred: this directly measures the official theme's optimization and benchmarking goals and mirrors Arm's published latency/throughput/memory analogues.]**
- **Llama + ExecuTorch compliance:** substantially yes, by asserting the model/runtime in the application path and capturing runtime logs/instrumentation; a dependency manifest alone does not prove the app uses it. **[inferred: static and dynamic checks can verify model/runtime presence and invocation, while source/architecture inspection confirms its role as the advisory component.]**
- **Offline operation:** yes, by disabling networking, tracing attempted outbound connections and confirming inference/advisory completes locally. **[inferred: a network-disabled test is a direct operational check of “without cloud dependency.”]**
- **Reproducibility:** substantially yes, with pinned versions, dataset/test hashes, deterministic evaluation scripts and a clean-device/clean-install rerun. **[inferred: those controls make reported results independently repeatable.]**
- **Explanation grounding and safety:** only partly. Automated checks can require evidence fields and constrain recommendations; humans still need to judge whether the explanation is faithful, understandable and safe in context. **[inferred: format and policy constraints are testable, but semantic faithfulness and user comprehension require review.]**
- **Novelty, social/product value, report quality, demo and presentation:** mainly human-scored, though completeness checklists and benchmark overlays can make evidence easier to verify. **[inferred: originality, practical value and communication are judgment-based; file presence and measured performance can still be checked mechanically.]**

## 5. Winner selection and student/professional comparison

The 2026–27 page says students and working professionals/developers “compete on equal terms across five problem statements” and that there will be “one winner for each problem statement.” **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Bharat_AI_SoC_2026_27.html + exact quotes.]** The natural reading is one shared winner slot per PS, not separate student and professional winners; no separate category winner is described. **[inferred: equal terms plus one winner per PS implies comparison in a shared PS pool, although the final selection mechanics are not specified.]**

The first edition was student-only and the official recap says 150 shortlisted teams were narrowed to five winners. **[sourced: https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Bharat_AI_SoC_2026_27.html + quote: “150 shortlisted teams were narrowed to five winners”; https://arm-education.github.io/Arm-Developer-Labs/Challenge_Page.html + Q1: “open to any undergraduate or post-graduate student”.]** It did not include a student-versus-professional comparison. **[sourced: same Challenge_Page URL + quote: “open to any undergraduate or post-graduate student”; no professional eligibility category is stated.]**
